I build and optimize high-performance scientific software in C++ and Python. My focus is the low-level work that makes numerical code run faster: profiling, parallelizing, and the careful engineering around it.

I hold an MPhil in Scientific Computing from the University of Cambridge (2026) and a B.S. in Applied Computational Physics, magna cum laude, with two peer-reviewed physics publications. I am based in New York City and open to software engineering, quantitative developer, and scientific computing roles.

**Highlights:**

- Optimized [CP2K](https://github.com/cp2k/cp2k/pull/5747), a major open-source simulation package: redesigned the OpenMP parallelization of its neural-network potential forces (42× over serial on 76 cores, merged upstream) and developed branches reaching 5.6× single-core and 2.6× full-node speedups, all bit-for-bit identical to the original output.
- Built a [Monte Carlo options pricer](https://github.com/ClaudioRMalvino/Monte-Carlo-Options-Pricer) in C++20 with OpenMP and pybind11 bindings.
- Published [Physics-TUI](https://github.com/ClaudioRMalvino/Physics-TUI), a terminal physics reference with 82 unit tests and strict typing.
- Currently building [Tensile](https://github.com/ClaudioRMalvino/Tensile), a C++23 header-only linear algebra library.
